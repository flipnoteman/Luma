The Luma Guide

Fourier transforms

Array<C32> (complex f32, see C32) has FFTs of any length along any axis, in one to three dimensions, batched over the remaining axes. They run on every backend as a few passes over the data, each transforming tiles in workgroup memory, and write the result in natural order.

use luma::*;

let x = Array::from_slice(&[C32::new(1.0, 0.0), C32::new(2.0, 0.0), C32::new(3.0, 0.0), C32::new(4.0, 0.0)], 4)?;
let spectrum = x.fft();                      // X[k] = sum_n x[n] e^(-2 pi i n k / N)
assert_eq!(spectrum.to_vec()?[0], C32::new(10.0, 0.0));
let back = spectrum.ifft();                  // divided by N: back == x

Normalisation

FftNorm says how a transform is scaled. The default of fft/ifft/… (without a norm argument) is Backward, the convention of NumPy and ArrayFire’s ifft:

None11
Scale(s)ss (once, however many axes)

N is the number of points of the transformed axis; each axis of a multi-axis transform is scaled by its own length.

Lengths

Any length works. Lengths whose prime factors are at most 31 (powers of two, 3, 5, 7, …) are transformed directly in as many passes as needed (one pass up to 4096 points per line, two up to about 16 million); other lengths use Bluestein’s algorithm, a circular convolution of the next power of two at least 2N - 1, which costs several times as much. Even-length real transforms are half-size complex transforms plus one pass that packs or unpacks the spectrum.

Convolution

fft_convolve2(&kernel, ConvMode::Same) zero-pads signal and kernel to a fast length, multiplies their spectra and transforms back. Same returns the signal’s shape with the kernel centred as ArrayFire does (output i is element i + k / 2 of the full convolution); Full returns signal + kernel - 1 along each convolved axis. The kernel must have extent 1 along the batch axes (2 and 3 for fft_convolve2). conv1d_fft is the one-dimensional full convolution with its own, convolution-specific passes.

A one-pass transform moves the array once in and once out; a transform of up to about 4096 points per line is one pass, up to about 16 million two or three. Results are accurate to a few f32 roundings: against an f64 reference the rms error is 3e-7 to 8e-7 of the rms of the spectrum (up to about 2e-6 at the worst point), a little above cuFFT’s.

Notes

  • The transforms are computed when called (they are not fused with surrounding lazy operations); their inputs are evaluated first.
  • A transform needs the whole array in one buffer: an array split over several buffers (see Performance) fails with LumaError::Chunked.
  • Errors poison the result like any other operation: fft_axis(4, ..) gives a poisoned array.
