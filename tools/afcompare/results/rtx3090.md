# luma vs ArrayFire 3.10.1 (CUDA 13.4): RTX 3090

Produced by `afcompare` (commit of this directory), steady-state job `afcmp-3090-final` and cold-start job
`afcmp-3090-cold`, both through the GPU queue on the local RTX 3090. Every row is its own interleaved (ABBA) measurement
of both libraries in one process; the two jobs are separate runs and are not mixed. luma = this repository at
origin/cpu-offload (171232f); ArrayFire 3.10.1 through the Rust `arrayfire` 3.8.0 C-API bindings.

Matmul TF32: ArrayFire's cuBLAS handle is switched with `afcu_cublasSetMathMode` (mode 3 `CUBLAS_TF32_TENSOR_OP_MATH`;
mode 1 `CUBLAS_TENSOR_OP_MATH` has the identical effect). The program probes that this really changes `sgemm`
(rms deviation from the default sgemm 2.6e-4, the TF32 size), so the TF32 rows compare TF32 with TF32. luma's default `matmul`
is TF32 on this GPU and is the same code path as the `matmul_tf32` rows.

Notes on reading the tables: "luma: N launches, X ms GPU" is a profiled call of luma (kernel timestamps), a check that
the timed closure does the real work. luma postpones and fuses `eval`s while the GPU is busy and never computes arrays
dropped before the GPU got to them (documented in `Array::eval`), so the timed luma closures keep their results alive until after the sync.

### First-call (cold) times

Cold = first call in a fresh process, after the library's own initialisation and an unrelated 400 ms warm-up kernel; median of 5 processes per cell, children interleaved. `cold`: all on-disk kernel caches empty or off; `disk-warm`: caches filled by an earlier identical run. AF TF32 includes the cuBLAS math-mode call.

| case | luma cold ms | ArrayFire cold ms | ratio (AF/luma) | luma disk-warm ms | ArrayFire disk-warm ms | ratio (AF/luma) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 elementwise chain, 8 fused ops, 16M | 11.49 | 30.07 | 2.62 | 0.6475 | 0.4120 | 0.64 |
| 4 matmul f32, 4096 x 4096 x 4096 | 77.23 | 39.38 | 0.51 | 6.983 | 42.66 | 6.11 |
| 4 matmul TF32, 4096 x 4096 x 4096 | 29.78 | 43.65 | 1.47 | 5.439 | 49.40 | 9.08 |
| 11 stencil: 5-pt Jacobi, 2048 x 2048, 100 iterations | 39.21 | 48.39 | 1.23 | 4.726 | 6.644 | 1.41 |

Cold-start remarks: the disk-warm ArrayFire matmul (about 43 ms) is not kernel compilation: it is cuBLAS handle creation and lazy
loading of the library's kernels, paid in the first call whatever the caches hold (the AF TF32 cell includes the math-mode call).
luma's cold matmul f32 (77 ms) is the compile of its own SIMT kernel; its cold elementwise (11 ms) and stencil (39 ms) are
tracing + NVVM + module load, against ArrayFire's NVRTC (30 / 48 ms).

## Where each wins, and why (RTX 3090)

luma faster:
- Launch/host overhead: 64 tiny fused ops 4.3x (one luma launch built and submitted in 20 us, ArrayFire 84 us for the same one fused kernel);
  32-op chain 1.30x; 1M 8-op chain 1.17x (both are launch-bound; at 16M/64M both sit at the memory bandwidth, ratio 1.00).
- Fusion / fewer passes: mean+var 4.5x (luma computes sum and sum of squares in one pass, ArrayFire runs mean and var as separate reductions);
  stencil loop 1.9-2.2x (luma replays the 100 steps as 40 launches with the shifts fused into the update, 16 launches when evaluated per step;
  ArrayFire launches 4 shift copies + 1 fused kernel per step; convolve2 variant 2.1x).
- Custom batched/segmented kernels: 4096 small matmuls 4.7x (one luma launch vs cublasSgemmBatched); sort of 1M rows of 64: 9.7x (ArrayFire's batched sort).
- FFT convolution 2.3-4.2x for a short kernel (luma runs overlap-save blocks; ArrayFire's `fft_convolve1` transforms the whole padded signal with cuFFT; algorithmic, not a
  faster FFT: with a kernel as long as the signal the gap is 1.39x). luma has no standalone FFT API, so cuFFT-class plain FFTs
  (ArrayFire alone: 1M 0.083 ms, 16M 1.38 ms, 2048x2048 2D 0.281 ms) have no luma counterpart.
- Data-dependent selection and scatter: filter 3.9x, where 2.6x (one fused count/scan/scatter vs where + lookup), gather 1.46x, cumsum 100M 1.41x and along the strided axis 1.2x;
  weighted scatter-add 8.4x (ArrayFire has no scatter-add: its side is sort_by_key + sum_by_key, a different algorithm).
- First call: elementwise chain 2.6x and TF32 matmul 1.5x, stencil 1.2x (cold, empty caches).

ArrayFire faster:
- Library sorts: f32 sort 0.78x (10M) / 0.76x (100M) (CUB/thrust radix sort vs luma's multi-kernel radix); sort_by_key 3.3x (luma has no native version: argsort + two gathers);
  argsort is a tie (1.01x).
- cuBLAS: f32 matmul 0.82-0.99x (8192: 0.90x, dense 256x1024x1024: 0.82x), attention P.V 2048x2048x64 0.57x (ArrayFire's SGEMM reaches 9.9 TFLOP/s vs luma's 5.7); TF32
  0.81-1.08x (1024^3: 0.81x; 4096^3 tie; 8192^3: 0.93x; Q.K^T and P.V slightly faster in luma).
- The MLP SGD step: ArrayFire 2.6x (f32) and 1.7x (TF32) faster: the step is dominated by small-M matmuls and transposed products, where luma's f32 SIMT GEMM and its 21-24 launches
  (0.55 ms GPU, equal to the wall time) lose to cuBLAS.
- cumsum along the contiguous axis of 4096x4096: 0.72x; histogram of 256 bins 0.88x.
- First call of matmul f32 (cold, empty caches): luma compiles its own SGEMM (77 ms vs 39 ms). Disk-warm, the order reverses (7.0 vs 42.7 ms) because ArrayFire pays cuBLAS initialisation.

Ties (within 3%): fused elementwise chains at 16M/64M (bandwidth bound), sum/max reductions 1M and 100M and both axis sums, select, bincount vs histogram, matmul 4096^3 TF32, argsort.
